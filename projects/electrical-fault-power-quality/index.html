Electrical Fault Diagnosis & Power Quality

INVESTIGATION 01 / 05

Repeated breaker trips

Record when the trip occurs, what loads are operating and whether the event is repeatable. Inspect for overload, short circuit, earth fault, defective equipment or an unsuitable protective-device arrangement.

Evidence to collect: circuit load current, insulation resistance where safe and appropriate, protective-device details and event history.

This is an investigation guide, not a fault-finding instruction for unqualified persons. Testing inside live equipment must be performed only by competent personnel using appropriate safe systems of work and correctly rated instruments.

The challenge

Intermittent trips, equipment resets, heating or uneven phase loading can have several possible causes. The symptoms may appear at different times, making it important to gather measurements and inspect the installation systematically before recommending corrective work.

Engineering approach

The work is approached in stages, with design decisions tied to the actual site conditions, equipment data and applicable requirements.

  1. Take a fault history and identify affected circuits, equipment and operating conditions.
  2. Carry out a visual inspection of accessible distribution equipment and connections.
  3. Measure voltage, current and phase loading under representative operating conditions.
  4. Use suitable test instruments, including thermal imaging or power-quality logging where justified.
  5. Identify confirmed defects and distinguish them from symptoms that require further monitoring.
  6. Complete authorised corrective work and repeat relevant tests to verify the condition.

Intended outcome

The intended result is an evidence-led fault report that identifies confirmed issues, recommends proportionate corrective action and establishes whether follow-up monitoring is needed.

Key engineering considerations

Safe isolation, instrument category and calibration, load conditions, harmonic distortion, voltage variation, thermal limits and test records.
